Citi

~20 hrs

Real-time status for the teams who depend on us

A live Confluence dashboard that gives upstream partners and requestors real-time status, instead of a daily email.

Before

Status was tracked by hand every day. Updates were chased in chat or in Jira comments, which were often empty or buried, then consolidated into a daily email. Our upstream partners and requestors never had a real-time view of their changes.

What I did

I onboarded my team to a dedicated stakeholder-update field in Jira with a standard format: pending defects, action owners and RAG status. Then I pulled it into a Confluence dashboard showing exactly what upstream partners and requestors needed to see.

Result

It saved about 20 hours a month and earned a Silver recognition. The dashboard went into everyday use, and our upstream partners adopted my approach to status updates for their own teams.

Citi

Early signals

The metrics package senior leadership reviewed

A recurring package I built and maintained, showing leadership how every project was really going.

What I built

I built the delivery metrics package for senior leadership and owned its maintenance. It covered milestone breaches with root causes, defects against SLA, UAT partner participation, sign-off time, hypercare incidents, and complexity and priority tagging.

How it ran

Project managers supplied the inputs, I maintained the package each cycle, and the team took turns presenting it.

Result

Leadership got one consistent view of delivery health across projects. It showed them early when a project needed their intervention, and just as clearly when it didn't, so escalations happened at the right time instead of too late or not at all. Together with the approval-records repository, it earned me a Platinum recognition from the Director.

Citi

Audit ready

One home for every approval record

A central repository of approval meetings, so approval evidence from years back is easy to find.

Why it mattered

Our projects were responses to regulatory concerns, and audits could ask for approval evidence from as far back as two years.

What I built

A central repository in Microsoft Lists that documents the minutes of every approval meeting: project name and ID, approvers, discussion points, artifacts and the related email correspondence. A semi-automated extraction in Excel then turns each record into a standard format that pastes straight into our emails.

Result

Approval evidence lived in one place and one consistent format, so we could answer audit requests quickly, even for projects closed long ago. Together with the leadership metrics package, it earned me a Platinum recognition from the Director.

Citi

3 months ahead

No more surprises in the books

A stakeholder dashboard showing every change coming in the next three months, and every one already implemented.

What happened

A change had to be rolled back after a data issue surfaced in production on implementation. The impact was material, so stakeholders escalated it to my manager.

What I built

Working with my manager, I built a dashboard for our stakeholders, end users and approvers. It shows the changes in their pipeline for the next three months alongside the history of changes already implemented. Before launch I held several consultations with them, so it fit how they actually work.

Result

Stakeholders could anticipate the impact on their books and judge whether they could take on the volume before changes landed. It earned me a Gold recognition.

Chevron

4,500 hrs

Simpler shift reconciliation for 26 stations

A faster shift close for Singapore retail stations, so customers weren’t kept waiting, without weakening accounting controls.

Context

I was the subject-matter expert, reconciliation coordinator and compliance champion for Singapore Retail Accounting. Every station closed each shift with a reconciliation of about 15 minutes, and the system couldn't be used while it ran. Staff couldn't serve customers, and customers had to wait for it to finish before they could pay.

What I did

Retail Business Consultants proposed simplifying station-level shift reconciliation, and I led the accounting workstream. I made sure the changes didn't alter how station data flowed into our accounting system, and didn't compromise how we investigated discrepancies flagged in our monthly reconciliation.

Result

About 4,500 hours saved across 26 stations, with less downtime at the counter and accounting controls intact. It also earned me a recognition.
